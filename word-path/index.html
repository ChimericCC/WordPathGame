How to play

Connect words. Claim tiles. Reach your end square before your opponent reaches theirs.

1Setup

Two players share a 26 × 26 board. Rows run A–Z from bottom to top, columns 1–26 from left to right.

Blue starts on A5 and must reach Z22. Magenta starts on A22 and must reach Z5.

Each player has 18 inventory spaces and 18 letters. Every starting rack can make at least one 6-letter and one 8-letter word.

2Opening phase

Both players get 35 seconds at the same time to build the longest word they can, in a straight line.

One letter, your master letter, must sit on your start square. Whoever builds the longer word moves first; ties are broken at random. Your first word is locked for the rest of the game.

3Turn flow

Each turn lasts 35 seconds. Choose one: play a word, exchange tiles, delete one of your words, or pass.

New words run left-to-right or bottom-to-top, with no gaps, and must touch your existing tiles. You can build through letters already on the board. Every word your tiles form must be real.

After you play, your rack refills to your inventory size.

4Tile exchange

  • Trade 2 tiles for 1 random tile: inventory shrinks by 1.
  • Trade 3 tiles for 1 letter you choose: inventory shrinks by 2.

Lost space doesn't come back on its own.

5Special squares

Purple spiral: cover it with a new tile to gain +1 inventory space (up to 24).

Gold star: cover it to gain a wild tile that can be any letter.

Each special square works once.

6Path and words

Your path is a chain of your tiles touching up, down, left or right. Diagonals don't count, and there can be no gaps.

7Shared board

Build near, across, or through your opponent's words. A tile used in both players' words becomes shared and counts toward both paths.

8Deleting words

Remove any word you played except your first. Tiles still used by another word stay. If deleting cuts your route, you'll need to reconnect before you can win.

9Win condition

The first player whose tiles form an unbroken path from their start square to their end square wins, and the current runs through it.
